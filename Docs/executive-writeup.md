# Alcovia assessment — executive writeup

I would use the first month to prove one useful student habit and make two existing operating decisions easier: which GTM campaigns deserve budget, and which students need attention. The submitted dashboards and data flows are **synthetic prototypes**, not claims that Alcovia's live systems are already connected. I would start by confirming source access, definitions and users' actual workflows, then pilot the smallest changes that can show a measurable result.

## What an academic advisor can do for non-tuition subjects

The advisor owns the **study process**, while the school teacher or subject expert owns explanations and grading. At intake, the advisor uses past marks, the exam calendar and the student's interests to agree a target for each subject and exam with the student and parent. If the school syllabus is unclear, they plan against a provisional quarter-by-quarter share and replace it when the actual syllabus arrives.

Each week, the advisor fits self-study around school, tuition and extracurriculars; names the chapters and time slots; and checks tasks completed, deadlines met, actual versus planned hours and evidence for each chapter-readiness stage. Each month, they compare syllabus coverage and exam scores with the agreed targets, review missed work and schedule a short catch-up sprint. A score deviation, repeated low practice accuracy or a blocked concept triggers a handoff to a teacher, tutor or suitable peer with the exact chapter and evidence; the advisor checks whether that help worked. The advisor does not certify subject mastery from a timer or a checked task. Missed-deadline reminders can be automated once identities, recipients and escalation rules are agreed; that production messaging is planned, not claimed as live.

# November workplan

I own the outcome, prioritization, five student interviews, pilot observation and Keep/Improve/Stop decision. The process/systems designer owns source definitions, low-effort staff inputs, unaided-use checks and operating handoffs. The full-stack engineer owns instrumentation, implementation, source integration, releases and recovery tests. One engineer works on one implementation stream at a time.

The [delivery workbook](https://github.com/co-DE-mon/Alcovia-assessment/blob/main/Docs/delivery-workplan.md) allocates 16 engineer days across November’s 21 weekdays, leaving five for holidays, leave, defects and interruptions. Release-review waits can shift pilot dates without requiring continuous engineering time. An [editable Excel version](https://github.com/co-DE-mon/Alcovia-assessment/blob/main/Docs/delivery-workplan.xlsx) is also available.

During 2–6 November, we confirm access and measurement, interview students and build the narrow Focus guidance. During 9–13 November, we test the app's interruption paths and release route and start GTM source validation. If approval, installation and usability checks pass, the Focus pilot starts on 16 November; the engineer then finishes GTM checks and connects the student dashboard. During 23–27 November, we finish the student-data and recovery checks, observe an advisor review and complete each student's seven-day Focus follow-up. On 30 November, I decide what to keep, improve or stop using user benefit, data quality and operating effort. Any delayed release moves the pilot and its outcome review.
